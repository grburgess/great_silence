# The Fermi Paradox

> "Where is everybody?" — Enrico Fermi, 1950

The Fermi Paradox is the apparent contradiction between the lack of evidence for extraterrestrial 
civilizations and the high probability estimates for their existence. Given the age and size of 
the universe, why haven't we detected any signs of intelligent life?

## The Numbers

Consider the scale of the cosmos:

The Milky Way is approximately 13.6 billion years old. Earth-like planets have existed for 
billions of years. If intelligent civilizations arise at even modest rates, the galaxy should 
be filled with them. Yet we observe... nothing.

The Fermi Paradox isn't just a curiosity—it has profound implications:

## Proposed Solutions

Over decades, scientists have proposed numerous solutions to the paradox:

### Rare Earth Hypotheses

- **Rare Planets**: Truly habitable planets may be far rarer than expected
- **Rare Life**: Abiogenesis (life from non-life) may be extremely improbable
- **Rare Intelligence**: Evolution may rarely produce intelligence

### Temporal Solutions

- **We Are First**: Conditions for life only recently became favorable
- **They Are Gone**: Civilizations don't last long enough to overlap
- **Timing Mismatch**: Communication windows don't align

### Detection Limits

- **Zoo Hypothesis**: They're watching but not interfering
- **Dark Forest**: Civilizations hide to avoid predators
- **Different Communication**: They use methods we can't detect

### The Great Filter

Perhaps the most sobering hypothesis: somewhere in the path from dead matter to 
galaxy-spanning civilization lies a nearly impassable barrier—**the Great Filter**.

<Aside type="caution" title="The Great Filter">
  If the Filter is behind us, we're lucky survivors. If it's ahead... we should be worried.
  Understanding where the Filter lies is crucial for estimating humanity's future.
</Aside>

## How Great Silence Explores This

Great Silence simulates the paradox computationally:

1. **Generate a realistic galaxy** with billions of potential habitable stars
2. **Apply Drake equation parameters** to model civilization emergence
3. **Simulate expansion** via self-replicating probes
4. **Apply hazards** (supernovae, GRBs) and extinction models
5. **Run Monte Carlo ensembles** to explore parameter space

By varying parameters, you can test different hypotheses:

```python
from great_silence import SimulationConfig

# Test "Rare Life" hypothesis
config = SimulationConfig.with_preset("early_filter")
config.civilization.fraction_develop_life = 0.0001  # Very rare

# Test "Technology Kills" hypothesis
config = SimulationConfig.with_preset("late_filter")
config.civilization.mean_civilization_lifetime_myr = 0.1  # 100,000 years
```
